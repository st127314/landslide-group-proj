# Methods, results and known issues

## 1. Study area and events

- **Study area:** 14 southern provinces of Thailand (`data/boundary/southern_thailand.geojson`), 1,055 tambons (GADM 4.1, level 3).
- **Landslide inventory:** 6,180 point events, 2000-03-14 to 2025-01-05 (`data/inventory/landslide_inventory.*`). Dates are satellite-image dates of scar-mapping layers, not occurrence dates (Known issue 2). All 23,284 southern landslide records, dated or not, are in `data/inventory/landslide_records_all.geojson` and are used to keep negatives away from real landslides.
- **Non-landslide points (paired design):** 6,180 pseudo-absences, **one per landslide** (`scripts/generate_paired_negatives.py`). Each is placed 2–10 km from its landslide (up to 22.5 km where the ring was full; 15% lie beyond 10 km), in the **same province**, at least 2 km from **every** one of the 23,284 landslide records and at least 250 m from the other negatives, and **takes its paired landslide's date**. The two classes therefore have identical year and province distributions by construction (checked: every pair matches on both), and share regional weather. Classes are 1:1.

## 2. Model 1 — landslide susceptibility

**Factors (19 used).** Terrain from ALOS AW3D30 V4.1 (SRTM 2000 for the earliest events): elevation, slope, aspect, general and profile curvature, TRI, TPI, LS-factor. Hydrology: upstream area, TWI, SPI from HydroSHEDS 15ACC (500 m) for events before 2020 and MERIT Hydro (90 m) for 2020 onward. Soil (OpenLandMap): texture class, clay, sand, bulk density. Vegetation: NDVI from the latest valid Landsat 5/7/8/9 or Sentinel-2 observation in the 90 days *before* the event day was extracted but is **no longer used by the model** (Known issue 14). Land cover: SERVIR Mekong RLCMS (previous year) with Dynamic World fallback. Lithology: GLiM level 1 (6 classes occur). Anthropogenic: distance to road and to river/waterway (OSM, 20 km data buffer). All temporal factors use only information from strictly before the local event day.

**Not in the model:** `dist_to_stream` (wrong scale before 2020, Known issue 5), `drainage_density` and `dist_to_fault` (98.5% and 60% blank), fault density and soil organic carbon (never produced). The fault layer is the GEM *active* fault database only.

**Training protocol.** 12,360 rows. Spatial blocks of 0.5° with a 5 km embargo around the hold-out fold; hold-out = one grouped fold chosen only from class share and size (2,628 test rows: 1,123 landslides, 1,505 negatives; 8,280 training rows, 4,284 / 3,996, after 1,452 rows were embargoed). Eleven algorithms were tuned with nested spatial cross-validation and the best nested-CV ROC-AUC chosen: **`logistic_regression_feature_selected`** (nested-CV ROC-AUC 0.918 ± 0.030; mutual-information selection keeps 18 of the 58 encoded inputs, C = 0.025; the selection step is seeded so a rerun gives the same model). Before NDVI was removed the selected model was `svm_rbf` (0.934 ± 0.028). An isotonic calibrator maps raw scores to calibrated scores; stored decision threshold 0.411 (raw) / 0.527 (calibrated).

**Hold-out performance of the selected model.** ROC-AUC **0.888** (95% CI 0.877–0.900), PR-AUC 0.806, balanced accuracy 0.804, sensitivity 0.980, specificity 0.628, Cohen's κ 0.573 at the out-of-fold threshold 0.411 (with NDVI: 0.940, PR-AUC 0.904). The confidence interval resamples points, but the test fold has only three spatial blocks (1,822, 477 and 329 points; one has no landslide), so it is too narrow. Resampling 0.1° sub-blocks of the fold instead gives 0.820–0.936 (`results/experiments/followup.json`, F4); treat the nested-CV spread (± 0.030) as the better guide (`models/susceptibility/model_metadata.json`, `results/figures/3_model_comparison.png`). Without NDVI the eleven models fall within 0.81 to 0.89 on the hold-out, and logistic regression and the SVM are within 0.003 of each other, so a simple linear model is enough. Selection used the nested CV, not the hold-out fold.

**Map.** The factor stack was point-sampled on a 250 m grid inside the boundary (148 of 150 tiles contain points; the other two clip the boundary with no grid point), scored and calibrated: 1,137,629 cells, 68.5% very low, 3.5% low, 5.4% moderate, 8.0% high, 14.7% very high (calibrated-score bins 0.2/0.4/0.6/0.8; 23.6% of cells exceed the decision threshold). Lithology and land cover are converted from the grid's numeric codes to the class names used in training before scoring. Output: `results/susceptibility_map/` (CSV and GeoTIFF; `scripts/make_susceptibility_png.py` renders the PNG the web map draws). **Two factor groups still differ between training and the grid (Known issues 11 and 15):** the grid's hydrology comes from MERIT Hydro (90 m) while 98.5% of training rows used HydroSHEDS (500 m), and the grid's road and river distances are blank in most tiles and wrong where present. NDVI, which also differed, is no longer a model factor.

## 3. Live rainfall forecast (GFS) — and the rainfall model that was dropped

**Live forecast** (`scripts/run_live_gfs_forecast.py`, about 10 s, no model). For each of the 1,055 tambons it sums NOAA GFS forecast rainfall over the next 24, 48, 72, 96 and 120 hours (5 days) **counted from now** (not from the model's initialisation time), using Earth Engine's `NOAA/GFS0P25` (`precipitation_rate` × the seconds of each step inside the window: hourly steps to +120 h of the run, 3-hourly after, so days 4 and 5 rest on coarser steps and a longer lead time and are an outlook; value of the 0.25° ≈ 28 km cell containing each tambon's representative point). Only the 24 h total drives the class. The 24 h total is classed two ways:

- **Local classes (drive the live impact).** Against the tambon's own wet-day rainfall percentiles: below P75, P75–P90, P90–P95, above P95. The thresholds come from `scripts/compute_rain_percentiles.py`: 30 years (1991–2020) of CHIRPS daily rain, averaged onto the 0.25° GFS grid so they describe cell-average rainfall like the forecast, wet day = at least 1 mm (53% of days on average), percentiles of those wet days, sampled at each tambon's point (`data/external/rain_percentiles_24h_tambon.csv`). Typical values (median tambon): P75 15.7 mm, P90 26.6, P95 37.6, P99 66.5; the wettest Andaman-side tambons need roughly twice as much as the driest to count as unusual. A wet-day P90 is exceeded on about 5% of all days, so "above P95" means roughly 10 days a year, not an extreme event.
- **Absolute classes (kept for reference).** Light < 10 mm, moderate 10–35, heavy 35–90, very heavy ≥ 90 mm (assumed thresholds, editable in the script). `RAIN_BASIS` in `build_impact_overlay.py` switches the overlay between the two.

Neither set is calibrated on landslide records. Local percentiles say **how unusual the rain is for the place**, not whether it triggers landslides; they are built from observed CHIRPS while the forecast is GFS, which has its own biases, so the two are only roughly comparable. Snapshot used in this document: GFS run 2026-11-10 00:00 UTC, window 2026-11-10 05:00 → 11-11 05:00 UTC; heaviest forecast 36.1 mm, median 5.3 mm. Against local normal 766 tambons are below P75, 188 at P75–P90 and 101 at P90–P95 (none above P95), whereas the fixed rule calls 384 "moderate" and 24 "heavy". On the quiet run of 2026-11-01 (max 14.3 mm) all 1,055 tambons were below P75. Output: `results/live_forecast/latest.json`.

**Why there is no machine-learning rainfall trigger.** An exploratory model was fitted on IMERG antecedent rainfall (`notebooks/rainfall_trigger_threshold.ipynb`, `models/rainfall_trigger/`). It has **no skill**: nested CV picks `random_forest` (CV ROC-AUC 0.74) but the spatial hold-out gives **0.34, below chance**, and all five candidates score 0.31–0.54. That is the expected outcome here: a negative shares its landslide's date and lies within about one IMERG cell (~10 km), so rainfall is nearly identical within a pair (median absolute difference 0.44 mm for 3-day and 3.0 mm for 30-day rain; median 30-day rain 55 mm for landslides vs 58 mm for negatives; univariate ROC-AUC 0.53 / 0.51), and the landslide "dates" are satellite-image dates (Known issue 2). The earlier non-paired design gave a hold-out AUC near 0.90 (XGBoost), which most likely reflected sampling artefacts rather than rainfall. The notebook and model are kept only as this documented negative result; **the live pipeline does not use them.** Calibrating a real rainfall threshold needs true occurrence dates.

## 4. Live impact overlay

`scripts/build_impact_overlay.py` joins, per tambon:

| Input | Use |
|---|---|
| Susceptibility grid (1.14 M cells) | % of cells high/very high → **terrain tier** (< 15% low, 15–40% moderate, ≥ 40% high) |
| `results/live_forecast/latest.json` | GFS 24 / 48 / 72 / 96 / 120 h rainfall, its class against the tambon's local percentiles (and the fixed class) |
| OSM buildings (426,519), OSM facilities (3,895), WorldPop 2020 constrained (9.39 M people) | each item takes the class of its nearest grid cell (≤ 400 m); counts per class and in high/very-high |

**Live impact class** = terrain factor × 24 h local rain-class index. Terrain factor: low tier 1, moderate 2, high 3 (tier from the share of the tambon's 250 m cells that are high/very high: < 15%, 15–40%, ≥ 40%). Rain index: below P75 = 0, P75–P90 = 1, P90–P95 = 2, above P95 = 3. Product bins: 0–1 low, 2–3 moderate, 4–6 high, ≥ 7 very high:

| rain \ terrain tier | low | moderate | high |
|---|---|---|---|
| below P75 | low | low | low |
| P75–P90 | low | moderate | moderate |
| P90–P95 | moderate | high | high |
| above P95 | moderate | high | very high |

It is a product, not a sum, on purpose: rain alone creates no risk on terrain that cannot slide (flat ground never exceeds "moderate"), no unusual rain means "low" everywhere, and "very high" needs the steepest tier **and** rain above that tambon's P95. (A first version added the two scores; the what-if scenario below showed that made every tambon "high" in a heavy storm, so it was replaced.) The rule is a **transparent triage rule, not a calibrated probability and not a validated landslide forecast**: the terrain half comes from a model with a held-out ROC-AUC of 0.89, the rain half from local rainfall percentiles (not calibrated on landslides). **People at elevated risk** = people living in high/very-high susceptibility cells of tambons whose live impact class is high or very high (same definition for buildings and facilities). Two sliver tambons contain no grid cell and are `not_assessed`.

**Results for the snapshot run (2026-11-10 00:00 UTC):** 11 tambons are *high* (5 in Phatthalung, 4 in Chumphon, 1 each in Trang and Nakhon Si Thammarat), 112 *moderate*, 930 *low* and 2 not assessed (slivers with no grid cell, attributed to Prachuap Khiri Khan); 1,024 people and 5 buildings are at elevated risk. On the quiet run of 2026-11-01 every assessed tambon was low and nobody was at elevated risk. The terrain tiers themselves (755 low, 193 moderate, 105 high) are unaffected by the weather. Independent of the weather, 55,669 of 9,385,884 people (0.59%), 3,947 of 426,519 buildings (0.9%) and 32 of 3,895 facilities live in high/very-high susceptibility cells; largest populations: Nakhon Si Thammarat 13,941 (1.0% of the province), Yala 11,464 (2.7%), Phuket 10,603 (1.0%), Ranong 4,015 (1.1%). Phuket, Nakhon Si Thammarat, Yala and Ranong have almost no landslide training points, so these are **extrapolation** (Known issue 3). GFS cells are ~28 km wide, so neighbouring tambons often share one rainfall value.

Outputs: `results/impact_overlay/impact_overlay_by_tambon.csv`, `..._by_province.csv`, `facility_exposure.csv`, `impact_overlay.json` (served by the API).

### What-if scenario (no extra model)

`GET /api/impact/scenario?rain_24h_mm=X[&province=NAME]` (and the "What-if rainfall scenario" panel on the Early Warning page) answers "what if X mm falls in 24 hours?" It replaces the 24 h rainfall of every tambon in scope (all, or one province; other provinces keep the live forecast) with X and re-applies the rule above to the stored overlay (`apps/backend/app/scenario.py`). It uses only numbers already in `impact_overlay.json` (terrain tier, each tambon's P75/P90/P95, exposure on high/very-high ground), so it needs no Earth Engine call and answers in milliseconds; with no parameter it reproduces the overlay's own classes exactly (checked: 1,055 of 1,055 tambons identical). Input is limited to 0–1,500 mm. Examples on the shipped data:

| Scenario (24 h rain) | Tambons very high / high / moderate | People at elevated risk | Buildings | Facilities |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| live forecast (GFS 2026-11-10 00:00 UTC, max 36 mm) | 0 / 11 / 112 | 1,024 | 5 | 0 |
| 30 mm, whole south | 1 / 153 / 782 | 21,977 | 1,172 | 20 |
| 50 mm, whole south | 82 / 213 / 756 | 38,809 | 3,471 | 22 |
| 100 mm (or 150, 250), whole south | 105 / 193 / 755 | 40,841 | 3,471 | 22 |
| 150 mm in Yala only (other provinces keep the live forecast) | 14 / 29 / 138 | 11,457 | 16 | 5 |
| 150 mm in Phuket only (other provinces keep the live forecast) | 1 / 17 / 122 | 5,009 | 850 | 6 |

Properties to keep in mind: a scenario applies the same rain to every tambon in scope (real storms are patchy); because classes are percentiles, they **saturate** once rain passes a tambon's P95 (26–69 mm in the south, median 38 mm), so 100 mm and 250 mm give the same answer; and the exposure counts only people, buildings and facilities on high/very-high susceptibility ground (40,841 of the 55,669 such people sit in tambons that can reach "high" or above).

## 6. Known issues (read before citing numbers)

Listed roughly by how much they could change a conclusion.

1. **There is no calibrated rainfall threshold.** The rain classes behind the live impact are local climate percentiles (how unusual) or assumed absolute thresholds, neither calibrated on landslides, and the exploratory ML trigger has no skill (Section 3). Two causes: the paired design removes between-class rainfall differences, and the landslide "dates" are image dates (issue 2), so even an ideal design would be fitting rainfall around the wrong day. A real threshold needs true occurrence dates. The live impact class is therefore a triage aid, not a landslide forecast.
2. **The inventory dates are satellite-imagery dates of scar-mapping layers, not occurrence dates.** The original workbook (`LANDSLIDE_INVENTORY_MAP.xlsx`, not included) has no event date, only `YEAR_DATA` (Thai year 2563–2568) and free-text `REMARK`/`REMARK_2` that name the mapping layer (`SNI_scar_ge.kmz` 2,262 events, `KBI scar13.kmz` 1,851, `Scar_CHP_New.kmz` 919, `PNA_Scar08022022.kmz` 911, `Scar_PLG.kmz` 144, `Scar_Trang.kmz` 92) and an image date; `date` was parsed from that string. The 6,180 points fall on only **164 distinct dates**, 86% on 15 dates (1,568 on 2017-03-04), 72% in February–April (clear-sky imaging season). An internal audit table (not included) shows that 6,178 of 6,180 dates equal a date in the remark, but every ambiguous slash string (1,784 events) was read month-first, while Chumphon (`Scar_CHP_New`: 204 day-first vs 1 month-first strings) and Trang (136 vs 0) are day-first, so **≈ 985 events (16%) probably have day and month swapped**, and 371 day-first records were dropped by the parser and are recoverable. Rainfall cut-offs are local-midnight proxies for all 6,180 events.
3. **Almost all dated landslide records lie in 6 of the 14 provinces.** Only records with a full date were kept (6,180 of 6,921 in those provinces); ≈ 16,400 year-only or undated records elsewhere were dropped (Nakhon Si Thammarat 8,596, Yala 3,964, Ranong 2,061, Songkhla 1,555, Phuket 95, Satun 72, Narathiwat 59, Pattani 46). Landslide points: Surat Thani 2,265, Krabi 1,843, Chumphon 918, Phang Nga 910, Phatthalung 130, Trang 110, Nakhon Si Thammarat 3, Ranong 1. Negatives now sit in the same province as their landslide, which removes the "which province" shortcut inside the sample, but the **map and exposure figures for the other provinces (notably Phuket, Yala, Songkhla, Narathiwat, Pattani, Satun, and Nakhon Si Thammarat/Ranong) are extrapolation**. Date information in the workbook for the 23,369 southern records: a full date for 28%, a bare year for 37% (all Nakhon Si Thammarat), nothing usable for 35%; `YEAR_DATA` cannot fill the gaps (digitisation batch year; equals the remark year for 1.3%). A static susceptibility model does not need exact dates, so the undated records could be added to it in future.
4. **Scores are relative and the task is easy.** Pseudo-absences are lowland points (median elevation 70 m vs 323 m for landslides; slope 7° vs 29°), so a high AUC largely reflects terrain contrast between mapped scars and random flat ground. Scores rank terrain under 1:1 sampling; they are not real-world probabilities, and AUC overstates real-world skill. Spatial CV with a 5 km embargo is stricter than random CV but does not remove this.
5. **Factors not used or still imperfect.** `dist_to_stream` is excluded because the HydroSHEDS branch (all pre-2020 events) returns ≈ 2.1×10⁷ instead of metres (only 178 rows, from the MERIT-era events, have sane values); the raw table still contains the wrong values and the extraction code was not changed. Upstream area, TWI and SPI come from the same two hydrology sources (HydroSHEDS 500 m before 2020, MERIT 90 m from 2020) but, because pairs now share dates, both classes use the same source for the same year, so this cannot act as a label leak. It does, however, differ from the grid, which is all MERIT (Known issue 11). `drainage_density` exists only for the MERIT-era events; `dist_to_fault` is blank beyond the 20 km search and uses active faults only; fault density and organic carbon were not produced. The team's internal planning document (factor list; not included) describes the plan; where the implementation differs (GLiM lithology instead of an ADPC asset, SERVIR land cover with Dynamic World fallback), this file is authoritative.
6. **Outliers kept as sampled:** SPI up to 6.7×10⁵, general curvature down to −334. Tree models tolerate these; the SVM / linear branches clip them (IQR winsorising fitted on training folds).
7. **Missing values** in the cleaned table: NDVI 446, soil 132, lithology 46, slope/aspect/LS-factor/TWI/SPI 17, land cover 10, upstream area 5 (imputed inside the pipelines). The imputers add missingness indicators, and missing soil values are more common among negatives (1.86%) than landslides (0.28%), so the indicators could encode the sampling process. They cannot affect the selected model: in the linear pipelines the indicators pass through the outlier clipping, which turns a column that is almost always zero into a constant, and with the indicators switched off the cross-validated ROC-AUC is identical to four decimals for logistic regression (with and without feature selection) and the SVM (`followup.json`, F4). A first check that showed a small difference (0.918 against 0.912) was comparing two runs of an unseeded feature-selection step; that step is now seeded. The tree-based models keep live indicators, and none of them was selected.
8. **Forecast uncertainty.** GFS is a global model on a ~28 km grid: it smooths local convective extremes and any single run can be wrong in timing and amount; each hour's rainfall is approximated as the instantaneous rate at that forecast hour × 3600 s; the local percentiles come from CHIRPS observations, so the comparison with GFS is approximate. Any numbers quoted here are a snapshot of one run; they are regenerated by `run_live_gfs_forecast.py` followed by `refresh_impact_overlay.py` (seconds, pandas only; reproduces `build_impact_overlay.py` exactly), which the server runs as a scheduled container job (`apps/forecast/`, every 3 hours once `apps/deploy/install-forecast-cron.sh` has been run).
9. **Exposure vintage.** WorldPop 2020 (constrained, UN-adjusted); OSM snapshot 2026-09-08 (completeness varies). Buildings, facilities and population inherit the class of the nearest 250 m cell.
10. **Not reproducible without credentials.** Earth Engine steps need a service-account key and project quota. Extraction was slow under quota (several requests timed out and were retried or split); the scripts are resumable and their merges are strict.
11. **Training and the map do not see the same hydrology (found in the final check, `results/experiments/followup.json` F2).** Upstream area, TWI and SPI come from HydroSHEDS 500 m for 98.5% of training rows but from MERIT Hydro 90 m for every grid cell (median upstream area 0.21 km² in training against 0.02 km² on the grid). NDVI differed in the same way (a pre-event value in training, quartiles 0.70 / 0.78 / 0.84; a current value on the grid, 0.34 / 0.57 / 0.73) until it was removed from the model (Known issue 14). Consequences: (a) the decision value reported in the progress report (87.9% of held-out landslides in the top 20% of the held-out area for the SVM, 65.4% for logistic regression) compared landslide *points* described by training-table values with *cells* described by grid values, and is not like for like; when each landslide is represented by the grid cell it falls in, the figure for the selected model is **60.0%** (plain logistic regression 58.9%, SVM 61.3%), about three times the 20% of a map without skill but below the 70% target; (b) removing hydrology, the distances or both changes this by less than one point, and terrain factors alone reach 61.9% (logistic regression) and 62.1% (SVM), so these groups add nothing to the map; (c) removing NDVI lowered the scores on sampled points (hold-out 0.92 to 0.89 for logistic regression) and did not change the decision value (58.7% with, 58.9% without).
12. **The model separates hillslopes from lowland, not failed from unfailed slopes (`followup.json` F3).** With both classes taken from the 250 m grid and described by the same static factors, ring-sampled negatives (the current design) give ROC-AUC 0.85–0.89, but negatives matched to the landslide cells on slope (±5°) and elevation (±100 m) give 0.49–0.51, and a model trained on the ring design scores 0.53–0.55 against the matched negatives. Matched negatives may include unmapped landslides, and 250 m cells blur a scar's own terrain, so this is a lower bound; it still means the map should be read as "steep, high ground like that where landslides were mapped", which is useful for screening exposure but does not rank individual slopes.
13. **Near-duplicate landslide points do not matter (`followup.json` F1).** Removing the 290 landslide points within 30 m of another one (and their paired negatives) changes cross-validated ROC-AUC by at most +0.002 and the hold-out by less than 0.003.
14. **NDVI leaked information from after the landslide and was removed.** The event date is the date of the satellite image on which the scar was mapped, so the scar already existed in the 90-day window used for NDVI. NDVI was the most important factor of the earlier SVM (permutation importance 0.060), and among points with slope above 20° NDVI alone separated the classes with ROC-AUC 0.75. The model was retrained without it: hold-out ROC-AUC fell from 0.940 to 0.888, which is the more honest figure. This also removes the NDVI half of Known issue 11. The 250 m grid was re-scored with the retrained model on 2026-11-14, and the map, the exposure and live-impact numbers and the files in `results/experiments/` were regenerated; `results/experiments/followup.json` (F4) holds the checks.
15. **The grid's road and river distances are broken (`followup.json` F5; found by a team review of the pipeline).** 119 of the 148 grid tiles have no road distance and 117 no river distance, so 77% of cells are blank and are filled by the median imputer. Where a value exists it does not agree with the training table at the same place: for 2,073 training points that fall in a grid cell with a value, the median road distance is 164 m in the training table and 11.6 km on the grid (Spearman −0.18), and the river distances are uncorrelated (0.06). The cause is in the grid extraction, not in the model. The effect on the map is small because the model leans little on these factors: without them the decision value is 59.0% (logistic regression) and 61.6% (SVM), against 58.9% and 61.3% with them. Fixing it means recomputing the two distances for the grid from the OpenStreetMap snapshot, or dropping both factors.

## 7. Reproducing a number

Every headline number comes from a file in `results/` or `models/`; `FILE_MANIFEST.csv` lists SHA-256 checksums so a reviewer can confirm they are looking at the files this document describes. `README.md` lists the pipeline order and `scripts/README.md` the commands for the extraction, forecast and overlay steps.
